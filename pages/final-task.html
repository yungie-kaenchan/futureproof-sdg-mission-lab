How this task works · วิธีทำภารกิจสุดท้าย

Read this first, before you start.

1

Read the situation below

You will be given a pretend scenario: you have been shortlisted for a one-million-baht Thai SDG grant. The grant is not real — it is a classroom simulation. Your job is to speak as if it were real, because that is how you learn to argue under pressure.

2

Pick one Thai audience

You will choose one real Thai institution to address — for example, the BMA Drainage Department or UNDP Thailand. You are speaking to them, not to your teacher. The platform records your choice; it does not send anything to the institution.

3

Use your evidence trail

The platform shows your six commitments from the six missions you completed — in your own words. Use any of them. Use all of them. They prove your argument was earned, not invented.

4

Record a 2–5 minute proposal

You can record live (audio or video) or upload a file. The system writes a transcript for you while you speak. You can edit it before you submit. Aim for 200–500 words in your transcript.

5

Submit when you're ready

Your work goes to your teacher for grading with Rubric A — the rubric is shown below, so you can see exactly how you'll be marked. Three AI judges will also give you advisory feedback right after you submit, but they never replace your teacher's grade.

One important note. The grant scenario is a simulation — no real money is awarded. It is the language, the argument, and the evidence that earn your grade. The Thailand SDG Catalyst Grant · 2026

ทุน SDG ประเทศไทย ประจำปี 2026 (สถานการณ์จำลอง)

฿1,000,000 — pretend grant
Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation กระทรวงการอุดมศึกษา วิทยาศาสตร์ วิจัยและนวัตกรรม (อว.)
UNDP Thailand โครงการพัฒนาแห่งสหประชาชาติ ประเทศไทย

Imagine that the Thai government and the United Nations have started a new grant programme. Each year, they choose one student team to receive one million baht for a community project that helps Thailand meet the Sustainable Development Goals.

Now imagine this: You have been chosen as a finalist.

Over the past weeks, you visited six Thai communities through this platform: Khon Kaen (the dry-season aquifer), Chiang Mai (the burning season), Bangkok (the klong and the flood corridor), the Andaman coast (the reef and the tourism quota), Mae Sot (the migrant children's schools), and the EEC fringe villages (the elder-and-child households). You read their stories. You heard the people speak. You made hard choices.

The grant committee will meet next Friday. They will not give the money to a slogan or a wish. They will give it to one proposal that follows these five rules:

  1. Speak to one real Thai institution by name — for example, the BMA Drainage Department or the Tak District Education Office. Not "the public." Not your teacher.
  2. Use what you learned in the six missions. Quote your own commitments from the journey. The committee can tell the difference between real evidence and a wish.
  3. Name what your action will not do. Every choice has a cost. The committee respects students who say the cost out loud — and refuses students who pretend it does not exist.
  4. Propose one action that fits inside one million baht. Be concrete: who does what, where, by when, with how much money.
  5. Use language the audience can act on. Polite. Clear. Not academic. Not a sales pitch.

You will not present in person. You will record a proposal between 2 and 5 minutes long — audio or video — and submit it with a written transcript.

This is a classroom simulation. No real money is awarded. What you are learning is real: how to argue for something that matters, in English that a Thai institution can act on. Take it seriously — that is the skill you will carry into your career.

— Dr. Payungsak Kaenchan Programme Director · FUTUREPROOF

What your proposal must contain · five parts

โครงสร้างข้อเสนอ — ห้าส่วน

  • 1
    The crisis วิกฤตที่กำลังกล่าวถึง What is the problem, and where? Say which community you are talking about — for example, "the families along Klong Toey in Bangkok" or "the elder households in EEC-fringe villages." In one or two sentences, describe what is going wrong for them right now. Do not be abstract. Name the place, name the people, name the trouble. Example opening: "In the Mae Sot district of Tak province, more than 2,000 migrant children attend community learning centres that the Thai system does not yet recognise…"
  • 2
    Your one action ข้อเสนอเดียวที่เป็นรูปธรรม What will you do, exactly? Propose ONE clear action — not three, not a list. Tell us who will do it, where, by when, and how much money it will take (inside the ฿1,000,000 envelope). The more specific, the stronger. Example: "Within 12 months, fund two bridging teachers in each of the 8 receiving Thai schools — ฿840,000 — and use the remaining ฿160,000 for shared learning materials."
  • 3
    Who it helps ใครเป็นผู้ได้รับประโยชน์ Be specific about the people. Not "students" in general — which students, in which schools, in which province? Say how their lives will be different in concrete ways. A grandmother who can now reach a clinic. A reef operator whose boat loan is not at risk. A child who can move from MLC to Thai school without losing a year. Example: "This helps roughly 600 migrant children currently enrolled in MLCs across Mae Sot district, ages 6–14, who would otherwise face a cohort gap during the phase-down."
  • 4
    The evidence หลักฐานสนับสนุน Use what you learned in the missions. Quote at least three of your six commitments from the journey (shown below in the Evidence Trail). The committee can tell when a proposal is built on real evidence and when it is built on a wish — using your own commitments shows you did the work. Example: "As I committed in the Burning Season mission, enforcement without alternative income is just a fine in disguise — that is why bridging staff, not deadlines, matter here."
  • 5
    The trade-off you accept ต้นทุนที่คุณยอมรับ What will your action NOT do? Every choice has a cost. Name it. Pretending there is no cost is the fastest way to lose the committee's respect. Saying "I know my plan does not solve X — and here is why I still accept that" is the fastest way to earn it. Example: "I accept that this plan does not reach the over-15 cohort this cycle. That is a real cost. I propose addressing them in Year 2, once the bridging staff are proven to work for the 6–14 group."
