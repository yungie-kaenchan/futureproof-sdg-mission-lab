## 1 · Project context (read once)

This is the **opening 60-second film** for an academic education-innovation
competition submission to *SPU TECH CREATIVE LEARNING AWARDS 2569 (2026)*
in Bangkok, Thailand. The submission is for a university-level English
language learning platform called **FUTUREPROOF — SDGs Mission Journey**,
created by Dr. Payungsak Kaenchan at the Faculty of Liberal Arts, Mahidol
University.

The platform sends Thai undergraduates on six adaptive missions across
real Thai regions, each anchored to a UN Sustainable Development Goal,
culminating in a Voice for Change capstone proposal addressed to a real
Thai institution.

The film you are about to assemble is the **first 60 seconds** of a
4 min 46 s competition video. After your 60-second intro, a live
screencast of the platform (already recorded separately by Dr. Payungsak)
will begin. Your job ends the moment Clip 6 fades to the screencast.

**Audience:** Thai academic competition judges (M.Ed. faculty members at
Sripatum University). They are serious people who watch many submissions.
Their attention budget is generous if the film signals credibility in the
first 6 seconds, ruthlessly short if it does not.

**Aesthetic touchstones:** National Geographic short film · Apple WWDC
keynote intro · Monocle quarterly editorial · Penguin Classics book cover.
NOT: corporate explainer · Canva-template motion graphic · stock-video
montage · neon / cyberpunk / synthwave.

## 2 · Goal of this 60-second film

Establish three things in the first minute, in this order:

1. **What the project is** (Clip 0 title card)
2. **The world it lives in** (Clips 1–2: Thailand, the question being faced)
3. **The platform itself** (Clips 3–6: the watercolor map, the six missions, the handoff to a live browser)

Total runtime: **1 minute 6 seconds** (6 s title + 6 × 10 s clips). Then
the film hands off seamlessly to the live-screen screencast.

## 6 · Per-clip detailed brief

### CLIP 0 · 0:00 – 0:06 · "Title Card"

**Story beat:** The film opens. The viewer sees the project name, the
tagline, and the closing promise — three lines of text on a cream paper
title page.

**Reference image to attach:** `ref-clip0-title-card.png` (an editorial
cream-paper title page with three lines of text already rendered)

**Motion prompt for Flow:**

> A static cream-paper editorial title page sits centred in the frame. The camera begins at a slightly wider shot and slowly pushes in toward the centre of the page over the full 6-second duration, as if the viewer is leaning closer to read. Subtle paper-grain texture is visible. Soft warm window light from camera-left creates the gentlest possible directional shadow on the page surface. Each of the three lines of text gently fades in one after the other across the first 4 seconds: Line 1 ("FUTUREPROOF — SDGs Mission Journey") appears at 0.5 s, Line 2 (the italic tagline) at 2.0 s, Line 3 (the small-caps slogan) at 3.5 s. The byline at the bottom fades in last at 4.5 s. Camera continues the slow push-in through the final 1.5 seconds, ending with the title filling two-thirds of the frame. NO music sting on screen. NO camera shake. NO cuts within the clip.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] All three text lines are present and spelled exactly as in the reference image (do not regenerate the text from a prompt)
- [ ] Camera movement is forward push-in, NOT lateral pan
- [ ] No new on-screen elements appear that were not in the reference image
- [ ] Lighting is warm and even — no harsh shadows, no spotlights
- [ ] Final frame holds steady ready for crossfade to Clip 1

### CLIP 1 · 0:06 – 0:16 · "Thailand at Dawn"

**Story beat:** Establish the world. Thailand at sunrise. Place, light,
breath. No people. No buildings.

**Reference image to attach:** `ref-clip1-thailand-dawn.png` (aerial photo
of rice paddies → river → distant temple at golden hour)

**Motion prompt for Flow:**

> Cinematic aerial drone shot, slow forward dolly across the Thai landscape at first light. The camera moves at low altitude over a misty rice paddy in Northeast Thailand, then climbs gradually to reveal a curving river (the Nam Phong), then a distant temple chedi catches the golden morning sun in the far background. Color palette: warm cream and gold highlights, soft pastel mist, deep teal river. Mood: intellectual, hopeful, quietly urgent — the opening shot of a National Geographic documentary. Camera movement is steady, unhurried, purposeful — no shaky cam, no fast cuts. 24 fps. Shallow depth of field on the rice paddy in the foreground; everything beyond softens gently into atmospheric haze. The final frame ends with the temple silhouette in the centre of the frame, ready for a held beat.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] No people in frame
- [ ] No power lines, no modern buildings, no roads
- [ ] Camera moves forward (dolly), not sideways (pan)
- [ ] The temple silhouette is visible in the far background by the final second
- [ ] No on-screen text

### CLIP 2 · 0:16 – 0:26 · "The Question"

**Story beat:** Introduce the protagonist — a Thai university student
weighing a serious question. Close, intimate, contemplative.

**Reference image to attach:** `ref-clip2-student-dossier.png` (close-up

**Motion prompt for Flow:**

> Slow push-in close-up on a young Thai university student's hands resting on a wooden desk in a sunlit classroom. The hands turn a single page of an open paper dossier — the typed text on the page is intentionally soft-focus and unreadable. A pen rests beside the dossier. The camera continues to push in, then very gently tilts up just enough to glimpse the corner of a thoughtful face in the upper-left of the frame, half-shadowed, eyes looking down at the page, not at the camera. Lighting: soft window light from camera-left, warm 4500 K. Color palette: cream paper, dark navy ink, warm honey-toned skin, soft brown wood grain. Mood: contemplation, weight of decision. Shallow depth of field. Quiet. NO computer, NO phone, NO logos, NO readable text.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The student's eyes are looking DOWN at the page, NOT at the camera
- [ ] Hands are in sharp focus throughout
- [ ] Face is partially visible but never the full subject of the shot
- [ ] No technology visible (no laptop, no phone, no smartwatch)
- [ ] No on-screen text

### CLIP 3 · 0:26 – 0:36 · "The Map Appears"

**Story beat:** The platform's visual signature emerges. A watercolor
Thailand is drawn before our eyes, six glowing pins appear one by one.

**Reference image to attach:** `ref-clip3-watercolor-map.png` (the
finished watercolor map with all 6 pins visible)

**Motion prompt for Flow:**

> A piece of cream-coloured watercolor paper lies flat on a wooden table, viewed straight down from above. Camera is locked in position; no movement. Slowly, as if drawn by an unseen ink pen in real time, the outline of Thailand appears on the paper in deep teal ink — first the northern borders (1.0 s), then the central plains (2.5 s), then the southern peninsula sweeping down (4.5 s). The lines flow naturally with the slight imperfections of a hand-drawn map. As the outline completes, six small amber-glow circular markers fade in one by one across the map (north, northeast, central, west, east, south) over seconds 5.5 to 8.0, each glowing softly like a candle. Color palette: cream paper, watercolor teal sea, amber-gold glow on the pins. NO photographic textures, NO 3D rendering, NO readable text labels. Lighting: soft overhead, warm, intimate. Mood: a quiet act of making.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The map's outline animates progressively — it does not appear all at once
- [ ] All six pins are visible and lit by the final frame
- [ ] Camera does not move (locked)
- [ ] No 3D-render appearance — must look like watercolor on paper
- [ ] No readable text labels

### CLIP 4 · 0:36 – 0:46 · "Six Frontlines"

**Story beat:** The viewer travels across the map. Each pin briefly

**References to attach:**
- Start frame: `ref-clip3-watercolor-map.png` (the finished map)
- Style reference: `ref-clip4-six-vignettes.png` (composite of six small painted scenes)

**Motion prompt for Flow:**

> Continuing from the previous shot of the watercolor Thailand map with six glowing amber pins. The camera now slowly dollies in toward the map. As the camera approaches each pin in turn (one every ~ 1.5 seconds, starting at the northern pin and traveling roughly counter-clockwise to end on the southern pin), the pin briefly blooms outward and reveals a small painted vignette beside it — each vignette is one of the six hand-illustrated watercolor scenes from the style reference: (1) a Northern temple silhouette in morning mist, (2) a Northeastern water tower in dry rice fields, (3) a Bangkok bridge over the Chao Phraya at dusk, (4) a border schoolhouse of bamboo and tin, (5) an Eastern industrial refinery with workers' homes nearby, (6) a Southern longtail boat moored beside a coral reef. Each vignette appears briefly, then dissolves back to the pin. Style: hand-illustrated watercolor and ink, never photographic. 24 fps, elegant pacing, slow continuous camera move. NO text labels. The final frame returns to a wide shot of the complete map.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] All six pins activate sequentially during the clip
- [ ] Each vignette is recognisably Thai and matches the style reference (not photographic)
- [ ] Camera moves smoothly, never cuts
- [ ] No on-screen text labels appear
- [ ] Final frame returns to a wide map shot ready for the next clip

The hand-painted map continues to live inside the browser window — the watercolor illustration is now a webpage. A small cursor appears in the lower-right and gently glides across the screen. Color palette: cream paper transitioning into the FUTUREPROOF browser UI (cream background, gold accents, dark navy header). Mood: an act of translation — from page to platform. Soft. Continuous. No cuts within the clip. Camera is locked. The final frame matches the layout of the end-target reference image.

- [ ] The transition is gradual — not a hard cut

## 7 · Assembly · transitions · audio

After all 7 clips are generated and approved, assemble in this order:

```
0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6
```

**Crossfade duration between consecutive clips:** 0.3 s
*(short enough that motion continues, gentle enough not to jar the viewer)*

**Audio direction:**
- **No voice-over on Clips 0 through 5**
- **Optional Thai voice-over on Clip 6 ONLY** (8 seconds, fades in over the
  hand reaching for the touchpad, line: *"หกภารกิจ หนึ่งแผ่นดิน หนึ่งเสียง
  ขออนุญาตพาท่านชมระบบกันครับ"*). Skip if no voice asset provided.
- **Score**: instrumental, royalty-free, cinematic but restrained. Mood
  should be *hopeful + serious*, NOT *epic-trailer-bombastic*. Suggested
  references: Max Richter "On the Nature of Daylight" feel, or Ólafur
  Arnalds piano-strings layering. AVOID: drum & bass, electronic, vocals,
  copyrighted music. The score sits at **−18 dB** for the first 50 seconds
  and ducks to **−22 dB** for the last 10 seconds in case voice-over enters.
- **Music must crescendo subtly toward Clip 5–6** and resolve to a held
  note at the end of Clip 6, ready to crossfade into the screencast's
  natural ambience.

## 11 · What to do if something is unclear

If any single instruction in this brief is ambiguous, default to the

more restrained, more "editorial" output) rather than the more
elaborate one. The aesthetic touchstone is *Monocle magazine front page*,

## 12 · Final note on tone

The competition this film enters is judged by senior Thai academics. The
single biggest risk is a film that looks *cheap or hurried* — bright
colours, fast cuts, cliché AI tropes, anime stylisation, neon glow,
copyright-questionable music, drop shadows on text. Lean into the
opposite at every decision point: slow, considered, photographic,
hand-illustrated, restrained, confident.

The reward for getting this right is a 60-second film that signals
*"This submission was made by a serious person"* in the first six
seconds — which is the only thing that buys the judges' attention for
the four minutes that follow.
