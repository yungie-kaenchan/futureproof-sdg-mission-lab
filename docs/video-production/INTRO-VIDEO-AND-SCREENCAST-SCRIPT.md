>   - **Part A** · Six 10-second Google Flow video prompts (60 s total) — an
>     editorial-grade intro that establishes FUTUREPROOF's objectives and

## 0 · Production overview

| Section | Length | What viewers see | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro · Clips 1–6 | 60 s | AI-generated editorial video | Instrumental score · no voice over (or optional VO line at clip 6) |
| Transition | 1–2 s | Smooth crossfade from Clip 6 → screencast | Music ducks |
| Screencast | 3 min 45 s | Live screen + presenter narration | Your Thai voice over the platform UI |
| Outro card | 5–10 s | Final URL + QR + creator credit | Music fades |

**Audio direction:** instrumental score throughout (no copyrighted music — use Descript's royalty-free library, Epidemic Sound, or Artlist). The intro's score should be **cinematic, hopeful, slightly building** — not anthemic. The screencast section's score should **duck under your voice to ~ −22 dB** so your narration is the dominant audio.

# PART A · Six Google Flow Prompts (60-Second Intro)

Each prompt is one self-contained block — paste each one into Google Flow individually. Generate, review, regenerate if needed. After all six clips are approved, stitch them in sequence in Descript / Premiere / DaVinci with smooth crossfades (~ 0.3 s).

**Shared visual language across all six clips:**
- Cinematic 16:9 (or 16:10 for cropping to A4-poster ratio later)
- Color palette: cream paper (#FDFBF6), ink black (#0A0A0B), gold (#C9A961), watercolor blues, soft pastels
- Mood: editorial gravitas · National Geographic short-film feel · NOT corporate brochure
- No on-screen text (Flow can't reliably render it — leave text for post-production overlays)
- No 3D render, no neon, no cyberpunk, no anime
- Camera moves: slow dolly, gentle pan, light push-in — NOT shaky cam, NOT fast cuts within a clip

## CLIP 1 · 0:00 – 0:10 · "Thailand at Dawn" (World-Building)

```
Cinematic aerial drone shot, slow forward dolly across the
Thai landscape at first light. The camera moves at low altitude
over a misty rice paddy in Northeast Thailand, then climbs to
reveal a curving river (the Nam Phong), then a distant temple
spire catches the golden morning sun. Color palette: warm cream
and gold highlights, soft pastel mist, deep teal river. Mood:
intellectual, hopeful, quietly urgent — like the opening shot
of a National Geographic documentary. The frame should feel
unhurried but purposeful. NO text. NO people in frame. NO modern
buildings. Vintage botanical-atlas color grading. 24fps, shallow
depth of field on the rice paddy in the foreground.
```

**What it does in the film:** Establishes Thailand as a place of stakes and beauty. Sets the editorial mood.

## CLIP 2 · 0:10 – 0:20 · "The Question" (The Challenge)

```

hour. Color palette: cream paper, dark navy ink, warm skin tones.
Mood: contemplation, weight of decision. NO computers, NO phones,
NO logos, NO readable text. Shallow depth of field. Quiet.

**What it does in the film:** Establishes the protagonist — a Thai undergraduate facing a serious question.

watercolor teal sea, amber glow on the pins. No text labels.
Lighting: soft overhead, warm, intimate. Mood: a quiet act of
making, an editorial unfurling. Camera is locked off, no movement.
```

**What it does in the film:** Introduces the visual signature of the platform — the watercolor Thailand map with six mission pins.

The watercolor Thailand map continues to fill the frame. Slowly,

## A.1 · Production tips for Flow

- **Generate each clip 2–3 times** and pick the strongest. Flow's first generation per prompt is often not the best — let it run twice and compare.
- **Save the seeds** of clips you like. If you regenerate, use the same seed to keep consistency.
- **If a clip renders text** (visible blurry letters in the UI mockups), regenerate with the appended sentence: *"All on-screen text must be intentionally unreadable / out of focus."*
- **If a clip looks photographic** when you wanted illustrated: append *"hand-illustrated watercolor style, NOT photo-realistic"*.
- **Color-grade the final stitched 60 s** with a single LUT in Descript / Premiere so all six clips share a warm-cream-and-gold tonality.

## B.2 · Recording tips for the Thai narration

1. **Record voice-over separately from the screencast.** Speak the script in one take into a quiet room with a decent USB mic (Blue Yeti, Shure MV7) or even your phone with a windscreen. Don't try to narrate while clicking — your voice will be uneven.
2. **Then record screen-only** (no voice) with Screen Studio, scrolling through Judge Tour at the pace you rehearsed. Save the .mov.
3. **In Descript, import the .mov and the voice file.** Drop the voice onto the audio track. Stretch / nudge clips on the video track so the screen action matches the words. This is way easier than trying to sync live.
4. **Use Descript's Studio Sound** on the voice — single click, makes laptop-mic audio sound studio-grade.
5. **For the intro:** drop the six Flow clips onto the video timeline in sequence, score underneath, and crossfade between clips at ~ 0.3 s.
