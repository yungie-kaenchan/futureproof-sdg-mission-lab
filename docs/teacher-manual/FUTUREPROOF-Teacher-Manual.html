CHAPTER 01

How to read this manual

วิธีอ่านคู่มือเล่มนี้
Two audiences are served by this single document. Read selectively — every chapter is self-contained, and the chapter opener tells you what's inside before you commit.

Who this manual is for

This manual addresses two readers with overlapping but distinct concerns:

  1. Teachers who will run FUTUREPROOF in a real classroom — what to do before, during, and after the course. The operational chapters (5, 7, 8, 10) are written for you. Time-to-first-class: ~ 90 minutes of reading + 30 minutes of setup.
  2. Competition judges and academic reviewers who need to audit the pedagogical claims, the AI–human boundary, and the architectural decisions. The theory chapters (3, 4) and rubric summaries (11) are written for you. Time-to-confident-assessment: ~ 45 minutes of reading.

CHAPTER 02

At a glance

ภาพรวมแบบเร็ว
Two pages. The fastest possible orientation. If you read nothing else, read this.

One paragraph

FUTUREPROOF is a self-contained AI-scaffolded web platform that sends advanced Thai undergraduates on six adaptive missions across Thailand, each anchored to a UN Sustainable Development Goal. Every mission runs the same five-stage arc — BRIEF → PROBE → DECIDE → ACT → DEBRIEF — and is scored by an AI Judge against an auditable rubric. Clearing all six awards six SDG Keystones and unlocks Voice for Change, the multimodal capstone proposal addressed to a real Thai audience. Throughout, an in-app Socratic field mentor (Mr Compass) provides language scaffolding within strict guardrails. The teacher remains the summative grader; the AI handles the formative scale.

Key facts

Course context
LALA109 · English for Digital Communication Skills · Faculty of Liberal Arts, Mahidol University · elective, advanced undergraduate (CEFR B1+ floor).
Cohort size
Up to 50 students per section × 6 sections = 300 max per term.
Term length
15 weeks · 3 in-class hours + 6 async hours per week.
Tech stack
HTML5 + Tailwind CSS + vanilla JS · Firebase (auth, Realtime DB, Storage) · an advanced AI language model API · Stability.ai for avatars · Netlify hosting.
First semester of use
Semester 1, Academic Year 2569 (Aug 2026).

The pedagogical spine, one sentence each

  • PICRAT — every mission lives in the Interactive-Amplifies cell or above; the capstone lives in Creative-Transforms.
  • AI-TPACK — AI handles consistency at scale (formative feedback, dossier tiering, language coaching); the teacher handles authority (summative grade, intervention judgment, cultural authenticity).
  • Three learning theories — Behaviorism (token economy), Cognitivism (5-stage chunking + CTML capstone), Social Constructivism (3-role asymmetric teams + Hall of Voices).

CHAPTER 03

Pedagogical foundations

รากฐานเชิงทฤษฎี
Two frameworks are load-bearing — remove either and the platform collapses. Nine more are supporting frameworks that fill in the specifics.

The two load-bearing frameworks

Most ed-tech platforms cite a long list of frameworks decoratively. FUTUREPROOF declares two as load-bearing — they govern every design decision, and removing either would collapse the pedagogical architecture.

PICRAT · the position on the matrix

Kimmons, Graham & West (2020) plot any educational technology on a 3 × 3 matrix: the learner's relationship to the technology (Passive · Interactive · Creative) by the technology's relationship to traditional practice (Replaces · Amplifies · Transforms). FUTUREPROOF targets Interactive-Amplifies as the floor for every regional mission, and Creative-Transforms for the Voice for Change capstone.

Practically: a mission that could be replaced by reading a textbook + answering a quiz lives in Passive-Replaces and is rejected at design review. A mission where the learner generates a defensible decision under a real trade-off lives at Interactive-Amplifies. A capstone where the learner creates a multimodal artifact addressed to a real audience lives at Creative-Transforms.

Load-bearing rule No feature ships if it sits in Passive-Replaces. This is the auditable test, and it has cut features from the design twice during development. See Reconstruction Master §2 for the rejected-features list.

AI-TPACK · the boundary

Mishra and Koehler's original TPACK framework asked which knowledge a teacher needs to teach with technology. The AI-extended version asks something sharper: what does the AI handle and what does the human handle, and why is that boundary the right one?

What AI handlesWhat the human (teacher / learner) handles
Formative scoring at scale (Rubric B)Summative grading (Rubric A)
Dossier reading at three CEFR tiersCultural authenticity vetting of dossier facts
Real-time language coaching (Mr Compass)Intervention judgment when a team is stuck
Consistency across 300 learnersThe voice of the learner in the capstone
Counter-position generation (DECIDE stage)Ethical arbitration of the trade-off itself
Load-bearing rule AI never gives content answers about the SDG topic. AI never overturns the teacher's summative grade. The capstone (Voice for Change) is graded human-only. Mr Compass redirects content questions back to the team. These are not aspirations — they are enforced in the system prompts and the platform UX.

The supporting frameworks (nine of them)

Bloom's Revised Taxonomy CAST UDL 3.0 Krashen i+1 Vygotsky ZPD Wood-Bruner-Ross Scaffolding Lave & Wenger Situated Cognition Hattie Visible Learning Mayer CTML UNESCO ESD

Each of these fills in specifics that PICRAT and AI-TPACK demand. Bloom maps the cognitive level of each stage. CAST UDL governs the three submission lanes for Voice for Change. Krashen i+1 sets the reading-tier algorithm. Vygotsky ZPD justifies the deliberate confidence trap at DECIDE. Lave & Wenger frames Hall of Voices as a Community of Practice. Hattie justifies the formative-feedback contract. Mayer CTML drives the multimodal capstone studio. UNESCO ESD provides the SDG content backbone.

The auditable test

For any new feature, three questions are asked in order. If a feature fails any one, it is redesigned or rejected.

  1. PICRAT cell. Where does it sit? If Passive-Replaces, redesign.
  2. AI-TPACK boundary. Is the AI/human split defensible? If AI replaces judgment that should stay human, redesign.
  3. Supporting framework. Which of the nine justifies the specifics? If none, the feature is decoration — cut it.
For the judge Ask any FUTUREPROOF feature this trio of questions. The platform's designers have answered them in writing for every shipped feature. If you spot one that fails the test, that is a fair finding and we want to know.
CHAPTER 04

Three learning theories, made operational

ทฤษฎีการเรียนรู้สามสำนักที่ปฏิบัติได้จริง
Behaviorism, Cognitivism, and Social Constructivism are the three classical schools every education faculty teaches. FUTUREPROOF doesn't just cite them — it maps each to specific platform features you can audit.

Why this chapter exists

Most ed-tech submissions cite Vygotsky once and move on. The premise of FUTUREPROOF is that the three classical schools of learning theory — Behaviorism, Cognitivism, Social Constructivism — are not in competition. They each model a different layer of how learners change. A well-designed platform operates all three simultaneously. This chapter shows which platform features execute each theory, so a judge or researcher can verify the claim feature by feature.

Behaviorism · Skinner · Pavlov · Watson

พฤติกรรมนิยม
  • Insight Token Economy — operant conditioning. Positive reinforcement +5…+25 for quality work; negative −5…−10 for weak reasoning.
  • AI Judges (3 evaluators) — immediate formative feedback within seconds, completing the stimulus-response loop.
  • Keystone Collection (6 dots) — variable-interval reinforcement schedule that sustains persistence across the term.
  • Adaptive Reading Tiers — successive-approximation shaping (Tier 1 → 3) so the learner is always inside their response range.
  • Verified Credentials — terminal reinforcer at capstone completion.

Cognitivism · Bruner · Ausubel · Sweller · Mayer

ปัญญานิยม
  • 5-Stage Mission Arc — chunking for cognitive load (Sweller). Each stage exposes only what working memory needs now.
  • Pre-Mission Brief — Ausubel's advance organizer. Three objectives + three outcomes + scoring criteria before content.
  • Mission Dossier — externalized schema. Read-anytime popup during Decide & Act reduces extraneous load.
  • Voice for Change Studio — text + audio + image (Mayer CTML dual-coding).
  • Integrity Meter & Time Capsule — metacognitive monitor (Flavell).
  • Consequence-aware decision branching — forces mental-model update (Bruner schema-construction).

Social Constructivism · Vygotsky · Lave & Wenger

การสร้างความรู้เชิงสังคม
  • 3-Role Asymmetric Team — Research Analyst, Comms Director, Ethics Officer; each sees different data. Forces co-construction in English.
  • Mr Compass — More Knowledgeable Other within ZPD; Socratic, never content.
  • NPC Stakeholder Interviews — situated learning in authentic Thai contexts (Lave & Wenger).
  • Hall of Voices — Community of Practice; peripheral → full participation.
  • Peer Judges (Tier 2 of 4) — peer rubric exchange; social mediation.
  • Stakeholder Dispatch videos — four real-Thai voices per mission, in-classroom co-construction.

Why this matters for scoring

Thai education-innovation rubrics typically award their highest marks under "การออกแบบเชิงทฤษฎี" (theoretical design) when a submission can show operationalised theory — not just cited theory. Every row above names a platform feature you can click on. Every theorist is named. Every claim is auditable in under 60 seconds with a live platform open. That is the standard we hold ourselves to.

For the judge If any cell in the triptych appears to overstate what the platform does, please flag it. The architectural commitment is that every theory citation in this manual maps to a feature in the live build — not to a plan, not to a future roadmap.
CHAPTER 05

Portal architecture

สถาปัตยกรรมระบบ
Six layers, top to bottom. What runs where, what data flows where, and how the AI–human boundary is enforced at every layer.

System layers

FUTUREPROOF · System Architecture
Presentation
Static HTML5 pages compiled with Tailwind CSS CLI (no CDN). Vanilla JavaScript ES2020+. Mobile-first responsive from 360 px upward. PWA-ready. Served from Netlify CDN.
Identity
Firebase Auth (email/password). Role flag on user profile: learner · teacher · admin. PDPA consent flags stored separately and gate any personal-data read.
Data
Firebase Realtime Database — learner profiles, decision logs, token balances, Keystone state, consent flags. Firebase Storage — avatar images, Voice for Change uploads, audio recordings. Security rules in firebase-rules.json enforce per-user write access.
AI proxy
Netlify Functions wrap an advanced AI language model API and Stability.ai. API keys live in Netlify env vars — never reach the browser. Per-call rate-limiting and per-user daily quotas.
Pedagogy
System prompts encode Rubric B logic (AI Judges) and Field Mentor guardrails. Every AI return includes a mandatory growthEdge — null fails validation and the call retries.
Content
Six SDG scenarios stored as immutable production masters in /scenarios/. Each scenario carries the dossier at three CEFR tiers, four stakeholder dispatch videos, the AI Judge scoring criteria, and the regional trade-off statement.

Data flows

  1. Sign-up → consent. PDPA consent recorded before any personal data write. The readiness check is hard-gated: it will not run and writes nothing until consent is granted.
  2. Readiness check → reading tier. Diagnostic sets the learner's CEFR tier (1, 2, or 3). Dossiers are served at the learner's tier; audio is never tiered.
  3. Mission decision → AI Judge → token award. Each decision call goes to the AI through the Netlify proxy with the Rubric B system prompt; the response includes scores per criterion, a growthEdge string, and a token delta.
  4. Keystone award. When the mission composite passes the threshold, the Keystone is awarded (idempotent — re-running cannot double-award).
  5. Capstone unlock. Six Keystones unlocks Voice for Change. Teacher overrides are logged with reason for audit.
  6. Hall of Voices curation. Capstone publication requires a second, explicit, withdrawable consent — separate from PDPA consent and revocable any time from My Data.

Why the architecture matters for compliance

Three architectural choices carry compliance weight that competition judges will look for:

  • API keys never in the browser. Every AI call goes through a Netlify Function. Inspect the page source; you will find no Anthropic key.
  • Granular consent flags. Photo, voice, decision-history, public-showcase — each is a separate flag, separately revocable. PDPA's data-subject-access-request workflow is a built-in screen, not a manual process.
  • 90-day auto-deletion. Personal data tombstones after 90 days post-course; pseudonymized analytics survive for research.

CHAPTER 06

The Thailand Journey · six missions

การเดินทาง ๖ ภูมิภาค ๖ SDG
One country, six regions, six trade-offs. Each mission anchors a different SDG to a real Thai context. Learners may take them in any order; clearing all six unlocks the capstone.

Why six, not seventeen

The UN has 17 Sustainable Development Goals. Why does FUTUREPROOF run six? Because six is the maximum a 15-week term can carry at the depth we insist on. Each mission has been authored to production-master depth — real Thai stakeholder names, real trade-offs, vetted illustrative ranges, four-voice dispatch videos. A 17-SDG version would be a mile wide and an inch deep. The roadmap (Chapter 13) describes how the platform scales to 17 once a partner faculty wants to commission additional regions.

CHAPTER 07

The 5-stage mission arc

วงจรการเรียนรู้ ๕ ระยะ
Every mission, every region, runs the same arc. The structure is a mastery spiral — not a linear textbook chapter — and each stage is anchored to a level of Bloom's revised taxonomy.

The arc, end to end

BRIEF — absorb the situation

The mission opens with the dossier delivered at the learner's CEFR tier. Same facts, different language. Four stakeholder dispatch videos play in any order. The learner is expected to surface the tension and whose interests it pits — not just summarise.

PROBE — question the sources

The learner is asked to evaluate source provenance (recency, methodology, conflict of interest) across the dossier. The AI Judge looks for whether the learner trusts the loudest voice or weighs provenance signals.

DECIDE — choose under trade-off

The highest-stakes stage. A real trade-off is presented; the learner commits to a position with confidence calibration. A deliberate confidence trap rewards calibrated humility and penalises overclaim — modelling intellectual humility in line with Vygotsky's productive struggle.

The confidence trap At DECIDE, the AI Judge does not score "correctness." It scores calibration. A learner who commits to a position with 100% confidence on weak evidence loses tokens; a learner who commits with 70% confidence and names the residual uncertainty scores higher. This is the most-asked-about feature by judges, and it is the architectural reason the platform does not collapse into "guess what the AI wants."

ACT — communicate the decision

The learner writes (or speaks) the decision to a specified audience — sometimes a community elder, sometimes a hostile policy panel, sometimes a hesitant SME. The Bloom level is Apply because the same reasoning must be re-cast across registers.

DEBRIEF — reflect on consequence

The strongest counter-position from PROBE returns, generated by the AI. The learner refines, refutes, or revises. Genuine revision scores higher than restatement. The DEBRIEF result drives the mission composite that awards the SDG Keystone.

Coach to the stage, not the Bloom level

A teacher walking between teams during a mission should coach to the stage in front of them. Some recipes for stage-specific intervention:

If you see…Try this prompt
BRIEF · team summarising not analysing"Whose interests does this pit against whose? Say it in one sentence."
PROBE · team treating all sources as equal"Which source has the most to lose from each version of the answer?"
DECIDE · team overclaiming"What's the strongest case against what you just committed to?"
ACT · communication too generic"Read your draft as if you were the audience. What would you push back on?"
DEBRIEF · team capitulating to the counter"You don't have to abandon the position — refine it. What did the counter get right?"
CHAPTER 08

AI-enhanced features

ฟีเจอร์ที่ขับเคลื่อนด้วย AI
Five named features make the AI side of the platform visible and auditable. Each is bounded by the AI-TPACK rule — AI handles scale and consistency; humans handle authority and cultural judgment.

Mr Compass · the Socratic Field Mentor

Mr Compass is the in-app Field Mentor — a Socratic companion, not a content tutor. Without strict guardrails this kind of chatbot would undermine the entire pedagogical architecture. The system prompt is engineered to:

  • Ask reflective questions that promote team discussion.
  • Provide language support only (vocabulary, grammar, register).
  • Offer encouragement and emotional support.
  • Suggest which team role might address a question.
  • Model professional English.

Mr Compass cannot give content answers, evaluate decisions, reveal earned-only information, predict consequences, or resolve ethical dilemmas. When asked for any of those, it redirects: "That's something your team needs to work through together. What does your Ethics Officer think?"

Mr Compass conversation panel
Three-message exchange: a learner asks for the answer; Mr Compass redirects to the team's Ethics Officer; learner reformulates as a language question; Mr Compass coaches a register shift.
futureproof-sdgs-lab.netlify.app/mission/compass · capture date TBD
Quota Mr Compass enforces ~10 messages per mission per learner. Unlimited use would degrade productive struggle. Language support is always free; strategic questions cost Insight Tokens (Expert Consultant, 15 tokens).

AI Judges · three formative evaluators

Each decision a learner submits during a mission is scored by three AI Judges working from the Rubric B system prompt. The Judges score per stage, per criterion, with 1 · 3 · 5 bands. Every return must include a specific, actionable growthEdge — a null fails validation and the call retries.

The three Judges differ slightly in instruction-following: one is calibrated stricter on factual grounding, one on language quality, one on ethical reasoning. The engine composes the mission pass-threshold from their combined output. Teacher audit on random-sample basis triggers prompt revision if drift exceeds 0.5 score-points.

CHAPTER 09

The Voice for Change capstone

ภารกิจปิดท้าย Voice for Change
Having walked all six frontlines, the learner proposes one concrete action to a real Thai audience. The capstone is multimodal, the audience is real, and the grader is human.

The prompt

The Voice for Change prompt is constant across all learners:

Having walked all six frontlines, propose one concrete action for a Thai community — addressed to a real audience, not the teacher.

Three submission lanes (CAST UDL)

CAST UDL Guideline 5 (Multiple Means of Action and Expression) is implemented as three submission lanes. The lane is a means of expression — it never changes the bar:

  1. Record live — in-browser, 60–180 s, unlimited re-record.
  2. Upload — a prepared audio or short video file.
  3. Create with Canva — opens a template in a new tab (never embedded), exported and re-uploaded.

Why the audience matters

The address is to the audience, not the teacher. A learner addressing the Khlong Toey community leaders writes differently than one addressing the Department of City Planning. Audience adaptation is part of what Rubric A scores under Critical & Ethical Reasoning and Craft of the Chosen Medium.

The evidence commitment

At each mission's DEBRIEF the learner commits to a specific piece of evidence they will carry into the capstone. The Voice for Change either uses that evidence or argues explicitly why it no longer holds. Rubric A's Dimension 4 (Synthesis & Evidence Commitment) scores this.

The teacher grades, not the AI

By design, Voice for Change is the one place AI does not score. This is the AI-TPACK boundary made operational: every formative mission was AI-scaffolded for consistency; the summative grade — the one that goes on the transcript — stays human.

For the judge If you take one architectural commitment away from this manual, take this one. The AI scales the journey; the human authorises the verdict. Removing this commitment would not save engineering time — it would unmake the platform.

Grading the Voice for Change with Rubric A

Rubric A is the rubric your transcript grade depends on. The workflow:

  1. Open the submission in Admin → Hall of Voices Curator → Pending.
  2. Play the submission once end-to-end without scoring. Form an impression.
  3. Score the five dimensions in the embedded scoring panel: Language Proficiency · Critical & Ethical Reasoning · Craft · Synthesis & Evidence Commitment · AI Collaboration Ethics.
  4. Add a one-paragraph qualitative comment.
  5. Save. The dashboard computes the composite and applies suggested band thresholds (38–45 A; 30–37 B; 22–29 C; ≤21 R&R).
  6. Decide independently of the AI Judges. Their scores are advisory.

Suggested grade weighting

Voice for Change · Rubric A
50%
Language growth · Rubric C
20%

20%
Mission decision aggregate · Rubric B audit
10%

You may re-weight on your syllabus. The Keystones are a gate, not a grade — earning six unlocks the capstone; they are never averaged in.

Auditing AI Judge scores

Random-sample 5% of AI Judge decisions across each cohort. Open the decision in Moderation, read the rubric criterion the AI scored against, and compare to what you would have scored. If you disagree, flag the decision; a daily report aggregates flags. Drift > 0.5 score-points on any criterion triggers a system-prompt revision in the next platform release.

PDPA + UDL compliance

การปฏิบัติตาม PDPA และ UDL
Two compliance regimes are built into the platform's foundations, not retrofitted. Each contributes architecture, not policy paperwork.

PDPA · Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act

  • Bilingual consent at sign-up — Thai + English, granular flags (basic profile · photo · voice · decisions · public showcase).
  • Hard-gated readiness check — the diagnostic does not run and writes nothing until consent is recorded.
  • My Data screen — every learner can export, correct, or delete their data at any time, self-service.
  • 90-day auto-deletion — personal data tombstones after 90 days post-course; pseudonymized analytics survive for research.
  • Audit logs — every admin access to personal data is logged with admin ID, timestamp, and reason.
  • Encryption — in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (Firebase default encryption).
  • DPIA — full Data Protection Impact Assessment is part of the submission package (docs/dpia.md).

Frequently asked questions

For teachers

Can I run with fewer than 6 missions?
Yes. The capstone unlock requires 6 Keystones, but a teacher override (logged, audited) can grant a Keystone for assessed offline work. Three missions + three offline-equivalents is supported.
A learner can't finish a mission. What do I do?
First option: have them replay DECIDE / ACT (Decision Replay costs 25 tokens, or you can grant a free retry). Second option: teacher override to grant the Keystone for offline-equivalent work. No learner is dead-ended.
Do I need to teach the SDG content first?
No. The dossier and stakeholder dispatch videos teach the content as part of the mission. Your role is to coach the reasoning, not the facts.
What if Mr Compass gives a wrong answer?
Mr Compass is designed not to give answers at all. If you see one, flag it in Moderation — the system prompt will be revised.

For judges

How do I verify a theoretical claim?
Every claim in Chapter 4 maps to a named feature. Open the platform, open the feature, and the claim is verifiable in under a minute.
What's the AI–human boundary?
AI handles formative scale (Rubric B, Mr Compass language coaching, dossier tiering). Human handles summative authority (Rubric A capstone grade) and cultural authenticity (scenario vetting). See Chapter 3 §3.2.
Is the platform original?
All scenarios, the 5-stage arc, the four-tier judging architecture, Mr Compass guardrails, and the Hall of Voices are original to FUTUREPROOF. The pedagogical frameworks are cited; their operational application is original.
What's the path to 17 SDGs?
Each additional region requires ~ 80 hours of scenario authoring (dossier × 3 tiers, four dispatch videos, AI Judge prompt tuning). The platform engine generalises; the content is per-region. See SUBMISSION-REPORT-v2.md §expansion.
