# Teacher's Guide

**FUTUREPROOF: SDG Mission Lab**
*Working draft — final Thai translation pending Day 18.*

This guide is the practical manual for teachers running FUTUREPROOF in their classroom. It is structured around the natural cadence of a course term — before, during, and after — and assumes you have administrative access to the platform and a class roster ready to onboard.

---

## 1. Before the Course

### 1.1 What you'll do once

- Provision your class in **Admin Command → User Management → New Class**. Enter the class code (e.g. `ELT-301-A`), institution, and term identifier (e.g. `2026-T1`).
- Bulk-import your roster as a CSV with columns: `displayName`, `email`, `yearOfStudy`. The system will email each student a sign-up link.
- Confirm class settings:
  - **Language Mode** — `english` (default) or `english+thaiSupport` (lower-proficiency cohorts).
  - **Cognitive Pace Mode default** — off unless you have specific accommodations to honor for the cohort.
  - **Token Inflation** — leave at 1.0 for first-time runs.

### 1.2 What students will do once

Each student completes the five-step onboarding:

1. **Sign up** — name, email, password, institution, year of study.
2. **PDPA consent** — bilingual flow with five granular flags. They can come back any time to update.
3. **Readiness check** — 12–15 minute four-section diagnostic. Calibrates their `learnerProfile`.
4. **Operator avatar** — photo upload or webcam → Stability.ai stylized portrait.
5. **Mission select** — pick an SDG → review three Claude-generated scenarios → lock one in for the team's run.

You don't need to monitor onboarding in real time. Check the Admin → Analytics tile the next day to confirm everyone is through.

### 1.3 Team formation

You can either let students self-form (3 per team) or assign them. Assignments are made on **Admin → User Management → Teams**. Each team must have one of each role — Research Analyst, Communications Director, Ethics & Policy Officer. Solo and pair players are accommodated; the platform fills missing roles with NPC consultants.

## 2. Running the Six Missions

Each mission has a roughly 90–120 minute live class footprint plus 1–2 hours of asynchronous team work. The pattern is consistent:

1. You hold a 10-minute briefing of the mission's cognitive demand (Bloom's level + what the rubric values).
2. Teams enter the mission and work through phases — briefing, decision points, AI Judge feedback.
3. You move between teams as a coach, not as the answer key.
4. The Field Mentor is available to teams in-app — Socratic questions only, never content.
5. End of session: 5-minute team reflection captured in Voice of the Learner.

### 2.1 Mission-by-mission

| Mission | Bloom's | Focus | What to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 RECON | Remember | Source evaluation, vocabulary, stakeholder map | Are teams choosing complementary sources or comfortable ones? |
| 02 DECODE | Understand | Audience-shifted explanation | Is register actually shifting between audiences? |
| 03 DEPLOY | Apply | Timed crisis response | Is the strategy traceable to evidence the team has? |
| 04 DISSECT | Analyze | Leverage point + counterargument | Are teams refining or just defending? |
| 05 TRIBUNAL | Evaluate | Ethical defense under cross-examination | Are they engaging the strongest counterargument or strawmanning it? |
| 06 FORGE | Create | Pitch Capsule | Does the artifact synthesize, or just sequence? |

### 2.2 The Field Mentor

Each team gets ~10 mentor questions per mission. The mentor cannot give answers. If a team feels stuck, your job is to ask them what they've tried — not to redirect them to the mentor.

### 2.3 Token-based scaffolding

Tokens are earned through quality decisions and spent on:

- **Expert Consultant** (15) — strategic AI question per mission.
- **Language Coach Session** (10) — detailed AI feedback on a draft.
- **Hint Reveal** (5) — one hidden data point.
- **Decision Replay** (25) — redo one decision.
- **Pronunciation Drill** (10) — AI-guided pronunciation practice.
- **Premium Design Template** (20) — upgraded Pitch Capsule layout.

Teams that spend strategically tend to perform better than teams that hoard. Surface this pattern after Mission 03.

## 3. The Pitch Capsule (Mission 06)

The Studio session typically runs over two class periods plus a week of asynchronous work. Most of your engagement here is at the rubric-A scoring stage after the artifact is submitted.

The five panels — Crisis, Journey, Insight, Solution, Voice — are role-led but collaboratively written. The Voice panel must be recorded narration, not text. PDF + audio export is one click.

After submission, students may consent to feature in the **Hall of Excellence**. Feature only if all team members have consented.

---

## 4. Grading

You are responsible for the **summative grade** — Tier 3 of the four-tier judging system. Your final grade is authoritative. AI Judge formative scores are advisory, useful for surfacing patterns, but never overturn your judgment.

The four rubrics:

- **Rubric A** — Pitch Capsule holistic (you).
- **Rubric B** — Mission decision quality (AI; you can see all decisions in the dashboard).
- **Rubric C** — CEFR-aligned language development (pre/post).
- **Rubric D** — Soft skills demonstration (you + peer judges in Mission 04).

Suggested grade weighting:
- Pitch Capsule (Rubric A) — 50%
- Language growth (Rubric C) — 20%
- Soft skills (Rubric D) — 20%
- Mission decision aggregate (Rubric B audit) — 10%

You can re-weight on your syllabus.

## 5. The Admin Command Platform

Seven modules:

1. **User Management** — accounts, classes, teams.
2. **Mission Architect** — add/edit scenarios for special cohorts.
3. **Analytics Dashboard** — engagement, decision patterns, language metrics, soft skills, Pitch Capsule trends.
4. **Moderation** — review AI content; flag and replay if a team got an unfair-looking judgment.
5. **PDPA Center** — consent tracking, deletion requests, audit logs.
6. **System Configuration** — model selection, difficulty parameters.
7. **Hall of Excellence Curator** — review and feature submissions.

All data is exportable as CSV / PDF for institutional reporting.

---

## 6. After the Course

- Students can return to **My Data** at any time to export, correct, or delete their data.
- Personal data auto-deletes 90 days post-course.
- The 30-day reflection (Voice of the Learner / Time Capsule) is automated — students get an email, you don't need to do anything.

## 7. Common Issues

| Symptom | First check |
|---|---|
| Team stuck on a mission | Ask what they've tried, not what they need. Resist the urge to answer. |
| AI Judge feedback feels off | Open the decision in moderation; replay against the rubric. |
| A student withdraws photo consent mid-course | Their avatar reverts to placeholder; nothing else changes. |
| A student asks to delete account | Self-service in My Data; takes effect after 7-day grace. |
| Teams stuck on Mission 05 cross-examination | The model is *supposed* to push hard. Drop in and reframe — "The model isn't graded, your reasoning is." |

---

## 8. The Pedagogical Spine

You are still the architect. The platform handles scale; it does not replace your judgment. Three reminders:

1. **Productive struggle is the point.** When students ask for the answer, they are also asking you to teach them to find one.
2. **The Field Mentor will not undermine you.** It is engineered to redirect to teammates and to you.
3. **Cultural authenticity is your domain.** The Local Lens is generated; its truth is curated by you.
