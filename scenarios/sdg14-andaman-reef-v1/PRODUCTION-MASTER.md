## 1.3 The empirical spine of the tension

This scenario is built on a **documented class of conflict**, not an invented one.

> The recurring, real Andaman pattern: a heat-stressed, contact-damaged
> reef inside a marine national park recovers measurably when human
> pressure is cut — Thailand has a well-known precedent of a flagship
> Andaman bay being closed for **multi-year reef recovery**, and of
> **visitor caps and seasonal closures** used as a management tool. The
> same instrument that helps the reef removes, in the **low season**, the
> *only* cash income of small dive operators, longtail/boat crews and
> island households who have **no funded alternative livelihood** when the
> dive sites close.

Plus, as qualitative spine facts (no invented precision):

- Reef tourism on the Andaman is **highly seasonal** — a high season and a thin low season; small operators carry debt (boat, gear, compressor) serviced from high-season cash.
- Carrying-capacity management is **established Thai practice**, but a closure or quota **announced before** a funded transition/compensation mechanism exists shifts the entire short-term cost onto the smallest, least-buffered actors.
- The ecological case and the livelihood case are **both evidence-backed**; the genuine dilemma is **sequencing and who bears the transition cost**, not "reef vs. greed."
- Recovery is **non-linear and partly irreversible**: a recovery window missed during a warm year may not return on the same terms — this is the intergenerational-stewardship axis, made concrete.

## 1.5 Flagged uncertainties — use ranges, never invent

The following are knowingly **not** specified to a precise figure. Where they appear, the dossier uses qualifiers ("around," "in recent warm seasons," "the department's monitoring describes," "reports describe"):

- ⚠ Exact current bleaching percentage on any specific Andaman reef — no single authoritative public figure used. Dossier: *"reefs across these islands have bleached in several recent warm seasons."*
- ⚠ Exact reduced-visitor quota number — fictionalised as a **40% cut to the daily visitor cap** plus **rotating closure of 3 of the most-stressed dive sites for one low-to-mid season** for narrative tractability. Flagged here as a **narrative choice, not a verified policy figure**. Acceptable because this is a *plausible decision case*, not a documentary.
- ⚠ Exact number of small dive operators / boat crews affected — kept as scale qualifier *"a few dozen small operators and the boat crews and island households who work with them."*
- ⚠ Specific dive-site, island, resort, or company names — deliberately not used.
- ⚠ Specific government-official or scientist names — deliberately not used.
- ⚠ Exact recovery timescale for these specific reefs — given only as a *months (colour) → years-to-decades (structure)* range, conditional on local-stressor reduction.
- ⚠ Exact size of any compensation/transition fund — deliberately unspecified; the *absence* of a funded mechanism is the point of the decision.

## 1.6 Cultural-authenticity guard

Stakeholder voice authenticity (per CLAUDE.md §11 + §15 and RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §2 dignity guardrails):

- **The small dive-operator / boat-crew family voice** is grounded in the real register of a working Andaman operator — practical, numerate about the boat and the debt, proud of the reef they show tourists, *not* folksy and *not* a victim. They understand the reef is sick; their objection is **sequencing and survival**, not denial.
- **The DMCR reef scientist** speaks measured technical Thai-accented English of the kind heard in Thai marine-science briefings — careful, hedged, evidence-bound, *not* an environmental absolutist who dismisses livelihoods.
- **The larger tour-company / resort operations director** speaks polished commercial English (investor-deck register) — articulate, not villainous; genuinely able to *absorb* a closure that would sink a small operator, which is itself the equity point.
- **The community-cooperative leader (bridging voice)** speaks plainly and structurally — translates between the science and the harbour, proposes mechanism, not blame.
- **Moken / sea-people ("ชาวเล") sensitivity:** the Andaman is home to Moken and other sea-people communities with deep, long-standing marine knowledge and their own relationship to these waters. They are referenced **only as legitimate marine-knowledge holders and affected coastal residents with agency** — never as an exotic backdrop, a tourism prop, or a costume. They are **not** caricatured, not spoken *for*, and not reduced to "tradition vs. modernity." Where the dossier mentions sea-people knowledge it does so to **widen whose stewardship counts**, supporting the intergenerational-stewardship axis.

**No stakeholder is positioned as "the wrong answer." All four positions are defensible from inside the speaker's situation.**

# 2. SCENARIO TEXT

## 2.1 Title

**The Reef and the Tide** — *แนวปะการังกับกระแสน้ำ*

## 2.2 Setting (28 words)

> On the Andaman coast, a marine national park's islands draw dive boats from small harbour towns. The reefs that bring the visitors have bleached in several recent warm seasons.

## 2.3 Core tension (22 words)

> A reduced-visitor quota and rotating site closures would let the reef recover — but cut the only low-season income small operators have.

## 2.4 Decision point (DECIDE stage)

> Should the marine park authority impose the reduced-visitor quota and rotating site closures this coming season — **before** any funded livelihood-transition or compensation mechanism exists for the small operators who will lose income?

## 2.5 Ethical axes (controlled vocabulary)

1. **Ecological recovery vs. coastal livelihood** — who bears the cost of giving the reef a recovery window (small operators, boat crews, island households with no off-season alternative) vs. who carries the benefit (the reef, future visitors, larger operators able to wait out a closure)?
2. **Intergenerational stewardship** — a recovery window missed in a warm year may not return on the same terms. Today's quota decision binds a generation of reef and of coastal families that has no seat at this table.

### 2.6.1 Part 1 — Why the reef matters

**Tier 1 (A2–B1)**

> On the Andaman coast, small towns live from the sea. Boats take visitors out to islands in a <vocab>marine park</vocab> to see the <vocab>coral reef</vocab>. The reef is why people come.
>
> But the reef is sick. When the sea gets too warm for too long, the coral turns white. This is called <vocab>bleaching</vocab>. If the heat stops soon, the coral can get its colour back. If the heat stays, the coral can die. Boats, anchors, and many feet in the water hurt it more.
>
> The park has an idea: let fewer visitors in, and close the most damaged dive spots for one season. The idea is legal. Whether it is fair is a harder question.

**Tier 2 (B1+–B2 — baseline)**

> Along the Andaman coast, small harbour towns depend on the sea. Dive boats carry visitors out to islands inside a <vocab>marine park</vocab> to see the <vocab>coral reef</vocab>. The reef is the reason the visitors come, and the visitors are the reason the towns have work.
>
> The reef, though, is under stress. When the sea stays too warm for too long, coral expels the tiny algae that feed and colour it and turns pale — a process called <vocab>bleaching</vocab>. If the heat eases in time, a bleached reef can recover its colour. If the heat persists, much of it dies. Anchors, fins, trampling and a high density of visitors add physical damage on top of the heat.
>
> The park authority has a plan: cut the daily visitor <vocab>quota</vocab> and rotate closures across the most damaged dive sites for one season. The plan is legal. Whether it is just is a different question — and the answer depends on whose situation you weigh first.

**Tier 3 (B2+–C1)**

> Along the Andaman seaboard, small harbour economies are structurally dependent on reef tourism: dive operators ferry visitors to islands within a <vocab>marine park</vocab> whose <vocab>coral reef</vocab> systems are the destination's principal asset.
>
> Those systems are under compounding stress. Sustained sea-surface-temperature anomaly drives the corals to expel their symbiotic algae and pale — <vocab>bleaching</vocab> — a condition that is reversible if thermal stress relaxes within the recovery window and lethal if it does not. Anthropogenic contact pressure — anchoring, fin and trampling damage, and the sediment and sunscreen load that accompanies high visitor density — is additive to the thermal signal rather than independent of it.
>
> The park authority's proposed instrument is a reduced visitor <vocab>quota</vocab> coupled with rotating closures of the most degraded dive sites for a single season. The measure is lawful. Whether it is equitable is a separate question — one whose answer is contingent on whose circumstances are weighted first.

### 2.6.2 Part 2 — Who depends on the reef

**Tier 1 (A2–B1)**

> Many small <vocab>operators</vocab> work here. They own one boat, or they work on someone's boat as crew. They take a few visitors out each day in the busy season. With that money they pay back the loan on the boat, the engine, and the air <vocab>compressor</vocab> for diving.
>
> The busy season is short. In the low season there is little work and little money. Island families also sell food, rooms, and rides to the visitors. They have no other job when the boats stop.
>
> Bigger companies and resorts also bring visitors here. They have many boats and savings. They can wait through a closed season. A small operator with one boat and a loan often cannot.

**Tier 2 (B1+–B2 — baseline)**

> Many small <vocab>operators</vocab> work this coast. Some own a single boat; others crew on one. In the high season they take a handful of visitors out each day, and from that cash they service the debt on the boat, the engine and the diving <vocab>compressor</vocab>.
>
> The high season is short and the low season is thin — little work and little income for months. Island households are tied to the same flow: they sell meals, rooms and transfers to the same visitors, and most have no alternative <vocab>livelihood</vocab> when the boats stop running.
>
> Larger tour companies and resorts also operate here. They run multiple boats and hold reserves; they can absorb a closed or capped season and reopen later. A small operator carrying one boat and a loan frequently cannot — the same closure lands on the two groups with very different force.

**Tier 3 (B2+–C1)**

> The operator base is dominated by small <vocab>operators</vocab> — single-vessel owners and crew — who, during the compressed high season, service capital debt on hull, engine and diving <vocab>compressor</vocab> out of a narrow window of tourist cash flow.
>
> Seasonality is severe: a brief peak followed by a protracted low season of minimal demand. Island households are coupled to the same revenue pulse through food, lodging and transfer services and are, in the main, without an alternative <vocab>livelihood</vocab> when boat traffic ceases.
>
> Larger tour companies and resort groups operate in the same waters but with multi-vessel fleets and balance-sheet reserves; they can amortise a capped or closed season and resume thereafter. The distributional point is structural rather than moral: an identical closure imposes a marginal inconvenience on the buffered operator and an existential shock on the unbuffered one.

### 2.6.3 Part 3 — Why the reef cannot just "wait"

**Tier 1 (A2–B1)**

> Some people say: the reef will fix itself, so we do not need rules. It is not that simple.
>
> A reef can get its colour back in a few <vocab>months</vocab> if the heat stops soon. But the broken coral structure — the shape that fish and the whole reef need — grows back slowly, over many years, and only if people stop hurting it during that time.
>
> Warm years are coming more often. A chance to recover that is missed in one warm year may not come back the same way. The reef the visitors love is also the reef the next generation should inherit. That is why the timing of the rules matters, not just the rules.

**Tier 2 (B1+–B2 — baseline)**

> One argument is that the reef will recover on its own, so strict rules are unnecessary. The science does not support so simple a reading.
>
> Colour can return within <vocab>months</vocab> if heat stress eases soon after bleaching. But the physical reef framework — the structure fish and the whole ecosystem depend on — rebuilds over **years to decades**, and only if local stressors are reduced during that window. <vocab>Recovery</vocab> is therefore conditional, not automatic.
>
> Warm seasons are recurring more frequently. A recovery window missed in one warm year may not return on the same terms. The reef today's visitors enjoy is also the reef the next generation is meant to inherit — so *when* the rules are applied, not only *whether*, is part of the decision.

**Tier 3 (B2+–C1)**

> The claim that the reef will self-rehabilitate, and that intervention is therefore redundant, does not withstand the ecology.
>
> Pigmentation may re-establish within <vocab>months</vocab> where thermal stress relaxes promptly post-bleaching. Structural <vocab>recovery</vocab> of the reef framework — the architecture on which fish assemblages and ecosystem function depend — proceeds on a years-to-decades trajectory and is contingent on the suppression of local stressors throughout that interval. Recovery is conditional and non-linear, not guaranteed.
>
> Under intensifying thermal-anomaly frequency, a recovery window foregone in a given warm year is not reliably recoverable on equivalent terms. The asset enjoyed by present visitors is simultaneously the inheritance of a generation absent from this decision; the *timing* of the instrument, not merely its existence, is therefore ethically load-bearing.

### 2.6.4 Part 4 — The decision before you

**Tier 1 (A2–B1)**

>
> Your team must brief the park authority before it decides. The authority will not accept "it depends." It needs a clear position, and reasons that hold up when other people push back.

The small operators have a genuine **survival need** in the low season — and **no funded transition or compensation mechanism exists yet** to carry them if their income is cut.

> Three claims are simultaneously valid: the authority's **statutory mandate** to impose a reduced <vocab>quota</vocab> and rotating closures; the reef's **recovery requirement**, which is time-bounded; and the small operators' **livelihood exposure**, which is acute and presently **uncompensated**, since no funded transition mechanism yet exists.

> Your team will brief the authority ahead of a binding decision. "It depends" will not be accepted. The deliverable is a defensible position with reasoning robust to adversarial challenge from the stakeholders.

## 2.7 Conflicting source pair — PROBE stage

Two short texts the learner must reconcile.

### SOURCE A — Tourism-industry association brief (211 words)

> ### Andaman Reef Tourism Operators' Association — Position Note, to the Marine Park Authority
>
> **The case against a quota-and-closure season for the upcoming year**
>
> Reef tourism is the economic foundation of every harbour town on this
> coast. The Association represents operators large and small, and the
> small operators are the ones a closure will break. Their high-season
> earnings already carry boat, engine and compressor debt through a low
> season in which there is almost no other work. A reduced visitor quota
> combined with rotating site closures, imposed before any compensation
> fund exists, transfers the entire cost of reef recovery onto the people
> least able to bear it.
>
> The reef's condition is real, but it is substantially driven by warm
> water — a factor no local closure can control. Penalising local
> operators for a thermal trend they did not cause, while no funded
> alternative is offered, is neither fair nor effective. Visitor pressure
> can be managed through mooring buoys, briefings and reef-safe practice
> without removing the visitors who pay for it.
>
> The Association asks the authority to **defer** the quota and closures
> until a funded livelihood-transition mechanism is in place, and to work
> with the Tourism Authority of Thailand on that mechanism first.
>
> *Prepared by the Andaman Reef Tourism Operators' Association, representing dive, boat and island-tourism businesses on the Andaman coast.*

### SOURCE B — Marine-science recovery study summary (220 words)

> ### Coastal Marine Research — Reef Recovery Working Note (with DMCR survey support)
>
> **Recovery prospects for the Andaman park reefs: a time-bounded reading**
>
> Repeat surveys across the park's most-visited dive sites show coral
> condition consistent with stress from both recent warm seasons and
> cumulative contact pressure; the two signals are additive, not
> independent. Sites with the highest visitor density show the slowest
> post-bleaching recovery.
>
> The argument that recovery is automatic does not hold. Pigment can
> return within months where heat eases, but structural recovery of the
> reef framework proceeds over years to decades and **only where local
> stressors are reduced during the window**. Mooring buoys and briefings
> reduce, but do not remove, contact load at high visitor numbers; on the
> most degraded sites, density reduction is the dominant lever available.
>
> Recovery windows are not fungible across years. Under rising thermal-
> anomaly frequency, a window foregone in a warm year is not reliably
> regained on equivalent terms. A reduced-quota season with rotating
> closures on the most degraded sites is, on the evidence, the
> intervention most likely to keep these reefs above the recovery
> threshold.
>
> The note does **not** address how affected operators are to be carried
> through the closure; that is outside its scope and is, explicitly, a
> question for policy.
>
> *Coastal marine-science working note, prepared with Department of Marine and Coastal Resources (DMCR) survey support.*

### Source-pair note for the AI applier

When scoring PROBE responses, the AI applier should treat **neither
source as simply "right."** The defensible reading is that **Source B is
stronger on the ecological claim** (repeat surveys, names the additive
mechanism, time-bounds the window, *and openly states its scope limit* —
it does not pretend to solve livelihoods) while **Source A is stronger on
the distributional claim** (it correctly identifies that an
uncompensated closure shifts the entire cost onto the least-buffered
actors, and that thermal stress is not locally caused). The trap to
detect: a learner who collapses the dilemma to "save the reef" *or* "save
the operators" without engaging that **Source B itself flags the
livelihood question as unsolved and out of its scope** caps at Band 2.
Learners who note (a) the additive-stressor / density-lever point in B,
(b) the cost-incidence point in A, and (c) that the real disagreement is
**sequencing**, not reef-vs-jobs, reach Band 4+.

## 2.12 DECIDE — tribunal / decision frame

> The marine park authority convenes a closed hearing. Your team must
> defend its position on the decision question:
> *Should the authority impose the reduced-visitor quota and rotating site
> closures this coming season — before a funded livelihood-transition or
> compensation mechanism exists?*
>
> The AI cross-examiner probes across **four rounds**, intensity
> calibrated to response quality (retry the DECIDE/ACT stage once before
> the score locks, per RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §3.1):
>
> 1. **Round 1 — premise check.** "You have argued X. What evidence is X resting on — the recovery science, the cost-incidence claim, or both?"
> 2. **Round 2 — stakeholder challenge.** "Stakeholder Y disagrees. Their strongest reason is Z. Answer Z — without dismissing the other side's evidence."
> 3. **Round 3 — temporal / stewardship challenge.** "Your decision binds a reef and a generation of coastal families not in this room. A recovery window missed this year may not return. By what authority do you bind them — and by what authority do you make this year's small operators pay for them?"
> 4. **Round 4 — concession invitation.** "Is there a premise in your position you would now revise — for instance, on sequencing or on who funds the transition?"
>
> **Defensible high-band shape (for the applier, not shown to learner):**
> the strongest positions do **not** pick "reef" or "operators." They
> resolve the *sequencing*: e.g. impose the recovery measure **conditioned
> on** a funded transition (cooperative-administered low-season work —
> mooring-buoy installation, reef monitoring, visitor briefing — funded in
> part by the larger operators who can absorb it and by TAT), with the
> closure scaled or phased so the reef still clears its window. A position
> that imposes the closure with **no** transition, or defers it
> indefinitely with **no** recovery safeguard, can still pass if it
> **honestly owns the cost it is accepting** and defends it under all four
> rounds.
>
> **Graceful Concession bonus:** teams that revise one premise *with
> reasoning* in Round 4 earn +10 Insight Tokens. Teams that collapse to
> "it depends" earn 0.
>
> **ACT artifact (next stage):** a 120–180 word adaptive-register brief
> **to the marine park authority** stating the position and its sequencing
> — register/word-count scales with tier (§3).

## 2.13 DEBRIEF — reflection prompt

> **Earn the SDG-14 Keystone.** Before the mission score locks, answer in
> 90–140 words (your reading tier sets the support, not the standard):
>
> *You have just decided who bears the cost of giving a reef time to
> recover. Name the one stakeholder whose position you found hardest to
> answer, and say honestly why. Then: if a recovery window really does not
> return on the same terms once it is missed, what does that obligate you
> to — and to whom — that a normal cost-benefit calculation would miss?*
>
> **Scaffold (Tier 1 provided · Tier 2 optional · Tier 3 hidden):**
> sentence starters — *"The hardest position to answer was… because…" /
> "If the window does not return, then I am obligated to… because the
> people who inherit this reef…"*
>
> This reflection is logged as the learner's **evidence-commitment** and
> is carried forward into the Final Task (§3.4).

# 3. ADAPTIVE & ASSESSMENT NOTES

## 3.1 Three-tier mapping (RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §5; D2 static tier-on-entry)

| Tier | CEFR band | Served when `learnerProfile.readingTier` = | What differs from baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | A2–B1 | 1 | Short sentences, one idea per sentence, minimal subordination, concrete verbs, high gloss density, scaffolds **provided** |
| **Tier 2** | B1+–B2 | 2 (baseline) | The reference register; moderate clause embedding; scaffolds **optional** |
| **Tier 3** | B2+–C1 | 3 | Nominalised, information-dense, embedded clauses, abstraction (e.g. "thermal-anomaly frequency," "cost incidence"), low gloss density, scaffolds **hidden** |

**Invariant across tiers (hard rule):** facts, mechanisms (heat stress +
contact damage are *additive*; recovery is *months for colour,
years-decades for structure, conditional on stressor reduction*),
stakeholders, hidden interests, the decision question, the source-pair
logic, and the correct scoring shape are **identical**. Only register
moves. **Audio is NOT tiered** — all learners hear the same stakeholder
dispatches; `.vtt` captions are the scaffold (SLA-correct asymmetry per
RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §5).

**Storage / serving:** content module exposes `dossier[part].body[tier]`
and `source[A|B]` (single register is acceptable for sources if a tiered
variant is not built; dossier MUST be tiered — it is, here, all 4 parts ×
3 tiers). `src/adaptive.js` reads `learnerProfile.readingTier` set once
at the diagnostic score step.

## 3.2 Gloss-density rule

- Gloss **text is constant** across tiers (the EN + TH gloss table at §2.6.5 is the single source of truth).
- Gloss **density** is tier-controlled by `adaptive.glossDensity()`:
  - **Tier 1 — high:** all 8 key terms glossed on every occurrence + the 6 supporting terms surfaced.
  - **Tier 2 — baseline:** 8 key terms glossed on first occurrence per part.
  - **Tier 3 — low:** only the 3 most technical key terms (`bleaching`, `quota`, `recovery`) glossed, first occurrence only; supporting terms not surfaced.
- Glosses never alter the sentence; they are hover/tap tooltips only (UDL: also keyboard-focusable, ARIA-described).

## 3.3 Pass-threshold → SDG-14 Keystone

- Composite mission score = weighted BRIEF (comprehension check) + PROBE (source-pair + stakeholder map + leverage point) + DECIDE (4-round cross-examination) + ACT (adaptive brief) + DEBRIEF (reflection).
- **Bar = all 5 stages completed AND composite ≥ 60%** (configurable in this scenario's manifest; default 60 per RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §3.1).
- Sub-threshold learners may **retry DECIDE/ACT once** before the score locks.
- On pass: **+1 SDG-14 Keystone** (binary, not graded), written to `keystones/{uid}`. Teacher override + `?demo=keystones` bypass behave per RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §3.2.
- Insight Tokens are earned per stage (process currency) and remain spendable in the scaffolding shop; Keystone is the narrative key (not spendable).

## 3.4 Longitudinal evidence-commitment → Final Task "Voice for Change"

- The DEBRIEF reflection (§2.13) is stored as this mission's
  **evidence-commitment**: *what the learner said a missed recovery window
  obligates them to, and to whom.*
- When all 6 Keystones are collected and the **Final Task — Voice for
  Change** unlocks (RECONSTRUCTION-MASTER §8), the SDG-14 commitment is
  surfaced back to the learner as one of six "things you said you owed."
  If the learner's Voice for Change proposal concerns a coastal / marine /
  livelihood-transition theme, this scenario's commitment is offered as
  the **continuity anchor** ("you said, after the Andaman, that…").
- This makes the journey's stewardship axis *longitudinal*, not
  per-mission — and gives the teacher (Rubric A holistic, AI-TPACK
  boundary preserved) a documented through-line to assess. Authentic
  audience (Hall of Voices, consent-gated) is the impact multiplier.
